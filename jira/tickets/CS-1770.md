## Description

## Summary

Today, every Caboodle user has one fixed role, such as "Brand Owner" or "Broker", and access to brands is tracked in several separate lists. This makes access hard to manage and easy to get wrong.

This work replaces that with a **company-based** model:

* Every brand and every brokerage becomes a **company** (an "organization").
* People **belong to companies** with a role (for example "Brand Admin at Acme" or "Broker at ABC Brokerage").
* What a person can see and do is worked out from **their company, their role and the brands they're assigned to**, every time they click.

This ticket covers the **backend** work. The web app and admin app updates are done by other developers in separate tickets, and everything goes live **together on one launch day**.

---

## Why we're doing this

* Companies can manage their own team (invite people, approve join requests, change roles) without asking Caboodle support.
* Brokers only see the brands they're assigned to, and only the modules their brokerage has.
* One person can belong to more than one company (for example, a consultant who works for a brand **and** a brokerage).
* New customers can sign up and create their own company.
* Every important change is recorded in an activity history.

---

## Who is affected and how

### Existing users

* Log in with the **same email and password** as today.
* Keep **exactly the same brands and modules** as before. Their current access is copied into the new model automatically, and nobody has to be re-invited.
* On launch day everyone has to **log in again once**.

| Today | After launch |
| Brand Owner  | **Brand Admin** of each brand they own  |
| Brand team member (sub-user)  | **Brand Member** of that brand, with full access  |
| Broker  | **Brokerage Admin** of their own brokerage  |
| Broker team member (sub-broker)  | **Broker** in their parent's brokerage, with the same brands as today  |

### New users

* **Sign up** with name, email and password (minimum 6 characters, typed twice).
* Get a **6-digit code** by email and must enter it **before they can log in**.
* After logging in, Caboodle looks at their email domain (e.g. `@acmefoods.com`):* If a company owns that domain → they can **request to join** it.
  * If not → they can **create their own company**.
* Invited people click the link in the email, **set a password**, and join directly. No code is needed, because the link proves the email address.

### Company admins (Brand Admin / Brokerage Admin)

* Invite people, resend or cancel invitations.
* Approve or reject join requests, and choose the person's role when approving.
* Change a member's role, suspend them, or remove them.
* Brokerage Admins assign their brokers to brands. Each assignment is either:* **Full**: every module the company has, including ones added later, or
  * **Custom**: a hand-picked list of modules and actions.
* Add their company's email domain and choose who can request to join: **anyone / people with a matching email / invitation only**.
* See a **recent activity** feed for their company.

### Platform Admin (Constance)

* Creates companies, suspends or restores them.
* **Connects brands to brokerages.** A brand can work with **more than one** brokerage.
* Switches modules on or off for each company. **A new company has no modules** until the Platform Admin turns some on.
* Creates **custom roles** for brand or brokerage companies.
* Can open any company to help ("support access"). Every change made this way is recorded and labelled as support.
* Sees the **full activity history** across all companies, plus platform-wide lists of members, invitations, join requests and domains.

---

## Key rules (what "correct" looks like)

1. **Admins always have full access** to their company's modules and can't be restricted.
2. **Brokers only get their brokerage's modules**, never the brand's own modules.
3. If a brand works with two brokerages, **each brokerage's own records stay private** from the other.
4. Removing someone from a brand, suspending their membership, or suspending their company **stops their access immediately**.
5. When someone isn't allowed to do something, they see a clear **"no access"** message. They are **not** logged out and **don't** see "Something went wrong".
6. **One company per email domain.** The domain must match the email of the person adding it.
7. **Free email providers** ([gmail.com](http://gmail.com), [outlook.com](http://outlook.com), [http://yahoo.com](http://yahoo.com) , etc.) can never belong to a company.
8. Every company must keep **at least one active person who can manage roles**.
9. Module permissions use six actions: **view, create, update, delete, import, export**.
10. The email log (in the admin app) is visible **only to admins**. This fix is already made.

---

## How it goes live

* Everything is built and tested on **staging** first. Nothing changes in production until launch day.
* Before launch, the full launch is **practised twice** on staging using a fresh copy of real production data. Each practice run includes going back to the old version, to prove that works.
* Before launch, every existing user's access in the new system is **compared with today's**. Any difference must be fixed or explicitly approved.
* **Launch day:** a planned maintenance window (customers are told in advance). The backend, web app and admin app go live together, and everyone logs in again.
* If something goes seriously wrong, we **go back to the previous version**. The old data is kept for 2–4 weeks after launch, so this is possible.

## Not included in this ticket

* Web app and admin app screen changes (separate tickets, other developers).
* Real DNS verification of company domains. Domain details are stored now, but checking them comes later.
* Locking an account after many wrong passwords (business decision: not needed now).
* Changes to the master password and the shared login key (business decision: kept as they are).

## Acceptance criteria

### Existing users

* [ ] An existing user logs in with their current email and password after launch.
* [ ] A brand owner sees the same brands and modules as before launch.
* [ ] A brand owner with several brands can switch between them and sees each one correctly.
* [ ] A broker sees the same brands as before launch, and only brokerage modules.
* [ ] A broker team member sees only the brands they were assigned to.
* [ ] Old invitation links that were still waiting to be accepted still work after launch.

### Sign-up and joining

* [ ] Sign-up requires a password of at least 6 characters, and both password fields must match.
* [ ] A 6-digit code is emailed; the user can't log in until it's entered.
* [ ] The code expires after 10 minutes; after 5 wrong tries a new code is needed; resend is possible after 30 seconds.
* [ ] After verifying, a user whose email domain belongs to a company is offered that company.
* [ ] A user with a Gmail (or other free) address is never matched to a company.
* [ ] A user can request to join; an admin can approve (choosing a role) or reject.
* [ ] A user can create their own company. They become its admin, and it starts with no modules.
* [ ] A user can create a company even if they already belong to another one.
* [ ] A domain already owned by one company can't be added to another.
* [ ] An invited person sets a password when accepting and lands in the right company with the right role.
* [ ] Invitations expire after 7 days and can be resent or cancelled.

### Access and permissions

* [ ] A Brand Admin and a Brokerage Admin always have every module their company has.
* [ ] A member set to **Full** gets new modules automatically when the Platform Admin enables them.
* [ ] A member set to **Custom** only gets the modules and actions picked for them.
* [ ] A broker assigned to Brand A can't open Brand B; they see a "no access" message and stay logged in.
* [ ] A broker never sees a brand-only module (e.g. a brand's Product Spec screen) through their brokerage.
* [ ] With Acme connected to two brokerages, each brokerage sees only its own records (market overviews, category reviews, brand reports).
* [ ] Suspending a member, removing them, or suspending their company stops their access right away.
* [ ] Ending a brand–brokerage connection removes that brokerage's access to the brand.
* [ ] Turning a module off for a company removes it for everyone in that company, admins included.
* [ ] The last person who can manage roles in a company can't be removed, suspended, or have their role changed.

### Platform Admin

* [ ] Can create a company (with or without a domain), rename it, suspend it and restore it.
* [ ] Can connect a brand to more than one brokerage.
* [ ] Can switch modules on or off per company.
* [ ] Can create, edit and delete custom roles. Built-in roles can't be changed, and roles in use can't be deleted.
* [ ] Support access works, and every change made during it is labelled as support in the history.
* [ ] Sees the full activity history and the platform-wide lists.

### Activity history

* [ ] Invitations, approvals, role changes, suspensions, brand assignments, module changes and connections are all recorded.
* [ ] Company members see their company's recent activity; only the Platform Admin sees everything.
* [ ] Passwords, codes and invitation links never appear in the history.

### General

* [ ] No "Something went wrong" errors appear where the correct answer is "no access".
* [ ] Only admins can open the email log in the admin app.
* [ ] Two full practice launches (including going back to the old version) pass before the real launch.
* [ ] The access comparison shows no unexplained differences between old and new access for any existing user.
