## Description

Brokerage customers currently have no real identity in the product — only individual users, loosely linked to whoever invited them. This causes three problems: we're a bottleneck for every access change, the rules for who can invite and who can grant brand access aren't consistently applied, and different screens give different answers to "who are my colleagues."

**Organization** makes this explicit. It's the brokerage itself, and it holds:

* a set of **brands** it's allowed to work on,
* a list of **approved email domains**,
* its **members** — Admins, Brokers, or Broker Sub Users.

The Primary Owner (us) creates the Organization and sets its brands. The customer's own Admin then runs their team — inviting colleagues and granting brand access — without coming to us.

**Roles:**

| Role | Who they are | What's new |
| Primary Owner | Us | Creates Organizations, sets brands and approved email domains |
| Admin | Customer's own administrator | **New role.** Invites colleagues, grants/removes brand access |
| Broker | Works on the brands they've been given | Can no longer invite or grant access |
| Broker Sub User | Works on the brands they've been given | Unchanged in practice |

**Two rules that run through everything:**

* Being in an Organization does not by itself give you access to any brand. Access is granted to a person, one brand at a time.
* What you're allowed to *do* depends on your role. What you're allowed to *see* depends on the brands you've been given.

**Scope:** Organizations and their brands, approved email domains, invitations, membership, and the switch-over of every area that currently makes its own access decisions — Category Review, Market Overview, Promotional Management, Distributor APL, Trade Spend, CRM, file libraries, brand navigation, and admin screens. Brand-side users (brand owners and their teams) are unaffected throughout.

**Rollout:** Twelve tickets. Ticket 1 sets everything up and everything else depends on it. Tickets 2–10 switch the product over one area at a time and are independent of each other — any order, or in parallel. Ticket 11 is the final central switch, covering everything with no protection of its own — released on its own with broad regression testing. Ticket 12 is cleanup, done in stages. Throughout tickets 1–11, existing customers should notice no disruption — old and new arrangements run side by side until the very end.

**Acceptance Criteria:**

* A brokerage customer can be set up in one action and thereafter runs their own team.
* Only the Primary Owner and Admins can invite people or grant brand access — enforced everywhere.
* A person cannot be invited using an email address outside the Organization's approved domains.
* Granting and removing brand access takes effect consistently everywhere (removal in particular is inconsistent today).
* Two brokerages working on the same brand can never see each other's work.
* Every existing customer is migrated automatically, with no loss of access and no manual setup.
* Every screen gives the same answer to "who are my colleagues."
* The old access arrangement is fully removed at the end.

**Release notices (flag to business ahead of ship):**

* Existing Brokers lose invite/grant-access ability at Ticket 1 — not promoted to Admin. Schedule the Admin-appointment pass alongside this release, not after.
* Automatic brand sharing stops at Ticket 9 — access now goes only to explicitly chosen people.
* Broker Sub Users see more distributors in CRM after Ticket 8 — corrects an existing inconsistency.
* When a member (or their brand access) is removed, their past work stops being visible to colleagues — deliberate, matches current behavior.
